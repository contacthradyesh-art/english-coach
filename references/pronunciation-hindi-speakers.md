# Pronunciation Practice for Hindi Speakers

This guide offers common areas to practise. It does not assume every Hindi speaker has the same difficulty. Use listening models from a trusted dictionary or teacher. A written transcript alone cannot confirm how a sound was actually spoken.

## 1. V and W
Some learners use similar sounds for **v** and **w**.
- Practise: vine / wine, vest / west, veal / wheel.
- Sentence: “We visited a very wide valley.”
- Tip: For /w/, round the lips; for /v/, gently bring the upper teeth near the lower lip.

## 2. TH and D/T
English has two “th” sounds that may be unfamiliar.
- Practise: think, three, thank, this, that, mother.
- Sentence: “They think this is the third thing.”
- Tip: For /θ/ and /ð/, place the tongue lightly between the teeth and let air pass. Do not replace every “th” with d or t.

## 3. S and SH
Keep **s** and **sh** distinct.
- Practise: sip / ship, see / she, sell / shell.
- Sentence: “She sells six shiny shoes.”
- Tip: /s/ is sharper; /ʃ/ (“sh”) is made with the tongue slightly farther back.

## 4. Avoid adding an extra vowel before S + consonant
A word such as “school” may become “iskool”.
- Practise: school, speak, special, street, student, stop.
- Sentence: “Students speak slowly at school.”
- Tip: Begin directly with the /s/ sound; do not add an extra “i”.

## 5. Final consonants
Do not drop the final sound.
- Practise: cap / cab, rice / rise, bad / bat, cold, asked, worked.
- Sentence: “I asked him to close the door.”
- Tip: Finish the final consonant gently but clearly.

## 6. Word stress
English words have stronger syllables. Stress can change how natural and understandable a word sounds.
- Practise: TAble, DOCtor, beGIN, imPORtant, deVELop.
- Sentence: “It is important to develop good habits.”
- Tip: Use a dictionary audio model to check stress; capital letters here only mark the stressed syllable.

## 7. Schwa /ə/
The schwa is a relaxed, unstressed vowel, often heard in the first syllable of “about” and the last syllable of “teacher”.
- Practise: about, support, teacher, problem, family.
- Sentence: “The teacher can support the family.”
- Tip: Do not pronounce every written vowel with equal force.

## Clarity routine (5 minutes)
1. Relax your jaw and shoulders.
2. Read one short sentence slowly.
3. Pause briefly at commas and fully at full stops.
4. Finish the last sound of each word.
5. Repeat once at a natural pace without rushing.
6. Notice fillers such as “um”, “uh”, and repeated “matlab”; replace them with a short pause.

## Feedback boundary
When only a speech-to-text transcript is available, comment on word choice, missing/added words, and sentence structure. Do not claim to assess actual pronunciation, pitch, voice quality, or accent without hearing audio.
